# TREATMENT — "Credit Card Free Mein Kyun? | How Credit Cards Make Money" (ExplainEveryday)

Style: **Sketchnote** (`sketchnote`). Why this style for this topic: the story is one money trail (₹10,000, then ₹150 of it) passing through four players, three income sources and a handful of rules. A sketchnote page lets one yellow thread follow the money across a single planned page, the numbers are hand-lettered on the page, and the paper-and-ink look matches the channel's cream hand-inked identity. Charts, slides and dark tech looks were rejected: they make fees look like a bank brochure.

Film: about 7:35, 1920×1080, 24 fps. Narration: the approved script, Roman Hinglish, read by an Indian-English **male** voice (Microsoft edge-tts `en-IN-PrabhatNeural`, online). Subtitles: Hinglish line over an English line. The host, ExplainEverydayHost (a boy), appears as a small ink-and-grey-marker doodle in his own corner of the page: curly hair, jacket, scarf shaded in marker (the style's one accent is reserved for the money thread, so no rust or navy). He points, shrugs and counts on his fingers; he never leaves the page.

Length note: the library's usual film is 30 to 60 seconds. This one is 7+ minutes and was agreed as the approved script; it is rendered in one pass.

## Three candidate structures

1. **A page in reading order.** Hook at top-left, then blocks left to right. Simple, but the camera only travels and the ending is a poster summary like every sketchnote.
2. **Before/after split.** "Pay full" down the left half of the page, "carry a balance" down the right. Clear on interest, but it leaves out the merchant side, which is the surprising part.
3. **One thread, one page.** The yellow thread is the ₹10,000 phone purchase. It is drawn leaving the card, travelling through issuer, merchant, acquirer and network, and a ₹150 slice peels off it. The same yellow then returns as the fat arrows of the other income blocks, and in the end the three rules are circled in yellow. The page is the money trail, built in front of the viewer.

**Chosen: 3.** It answers the opening question ("bank interest ke bina kaise kamata hai?") in the shape of the drawing: you can see the money leave the customer and land in four hands. The "one colour as the thread" move is the style's own native move, and the hook, the "interest" detour and the ending all come back to the same ₹10,000.

## Logline and arc
A doodle boy follows ₹10,000 through a card payment on one sheet of dot-grid paper and shows that the bank earns even when you pay on time.
- **Setup (0:00–0:40):** you pay on time; the bank gets no interest; so why is the card free?
- **Turn (0:40–3:05):** the money trail and the ₹150 merchant fee, only part of which reaches the issuing bank.
- **Development (3:05–6:05):** the other income sources (interest, fees, rewards that are funded by the system).
- **Ending (6:05–7:35):** profit is not guaranteed; the poster pulls back; the three rules are circled; sign-off.

## Benchmark
- **Learn:** the pen as the camera; one accent colour that follows one thing; numbers written large and circled; micro notes read only on a dive.
- **Don't take:** any live-sketchnote layouts or icons, the demo's honey-making story, its page layout or its icons.

## Facts on the page
The ₹10,000 / 1.5% / ₹150 / ₹9,850 example is labelled "illustrative, not a standard MDR rate" and the ₹150 is split into three unlabelled-size slices (acquirer or payment provider, card network, interchange to the issuing bank) with "split varies". No share of the fee is invented. The film never says the whole ₹150 goes to the card-issuing bank. See `FACTS.md`.

## Cue map
Tempo 84 bpm, bar 2.857 s; section starts snap to the nearest bar once the voice is rendered. Pen strokes (foley) follow the stroke events, ₹ numbers land on a marimba note, and the ₹150 reveal lands on a pitched pluck.
