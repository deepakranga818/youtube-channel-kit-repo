# TREATMENT — "अदृश्य ब्रह्मांड का नक्शा" (Mapping the Invisible Universe)

Style: Scale Zoom Explainer (`flat-space-explainer`). Film: about 2:57, 1920×1080, 24 fps. Narration: Hindi (Devanagari), Microsoft edge-tts voice (online). Subtitles: Hindi line over an English line. Topic: how scientists map dark matter without seeing it, prompted by ESA's first major Euclid data release (12 Nov 2026).

## Three candidate structures

1. **Outward journey.** Start on Earth, zoom out through Euclid's orbit, the Milky Way and clusters to the cosmic web, ending on the biggest picture. Clear, but it is the style's default cliché and the ending is just "big".
2. **Inward journey.** Start on the cosmic web and dive into one galaxy. It buries the "invisible" idea under scale.
3. **Follow one ray of light.** A single dot of light from a distant galaxy is the sight line: it crosses the dark halo of a massive cluster, bends, and arrives at a telescope at L2. The scale ribbon runs from the far end of the universe to a dish in India, and the guide tells the story by following the line. The ring, the arcs and the weak-lensing map are all the same light seen at different distances.

**Chosen: 3.** The topic is literally about reading what a light path does on its way to us. The style's native "sight line" becomes the evidence, so the film's own camera idea is the explanation. The opening (a black frame with one dot that turns out to be a galaxy) and ending (the ribbon with every stop lit, then the dot again) bookend it.

## Logline and arc
A tiny guide follows one dotted line of light from a far galaxy to a telescope and shows that dark matter is never seen, only measured by what it does to the line.
- **Setup:** a dot is a galaxy; Euclid has catalogued 900 million+ of them; most of the universe's matter is something we cannot see.
- **Turn:** the sight line bends around an unseen halo, and the bend is the evidence (an Einstein ring, then ~15,000 candidate lenses).
- **Ending:** the ribbon summary lights every stop; the takeaway is that an invisible thing is known by its effects; the guide returns to the single dot.

## Benchmark
- **Learn:** one continuous change of scale with a readout; a guide who points; one label or one number per screen; true size shown once.
- **Don't take:** any poster's characters, layouts, type or music; the demo's story, shots or guide.

## Guide ("तारा", Tara)
A small round sun-yellow comet: round head, a short two-shade tail that trails behind, coral cheeks, one cream-ringed lens for an eye. Drawn larger than life; state once that it is drawn bigger than real, and show its true size once (at the Earth level).

## Facts used (see FACTS.md)
900M+ galaxies catalogued in the data release (catalogued, not newly discovered); about 15,000 strong-lens candidates (candidates, not all confirmed); about 13 months of observations; an initial dataset, not the finished 3D map, with the full first data release expected mid-2027; dark matter is inferred from gravity and has not been photographed; dark energy is a separate mystery tied to cosmic expansion; GMRT is a different instrument that Euclid does not use.
